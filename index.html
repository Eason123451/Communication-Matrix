How to use this page
  1. Work through Levels 1–8 in order. Each level adds one new idea, starting with just two people.
  2. Read the Learn box, then do Your task on the network. To draw an arrow, click a person, then click who they can message. Click the same pair again to remove it.
  3. When the task shows ✓ done, answer the question and press Check. A correct answer unlocks the next level.
  4. Watch the matrices on the right change as you draw. You can also click a number in C to switch a link on or off, and click a number in C² to see the routes it counts.
  5. Free play (after Level 8) gives you ready-made scenarios, up to 6 people, the full explanation and unlimited practice questions.

Teachers: use Presentation mode to enlarge the text on a projector. Every level can be opened directly from the level bar.

Model assumptions & teaching connections

How the model works

  • C is a square binary matrix (only 0s and 1s). Element cij = 1 means person i can send a message directly to person j.
  • With “Two-way links” on, every link you draw goes both ways, so C is symmetric. Turn it off to model one-way communication (e.g. a manager who sends but doesn’t receive).
  • C² counts routes through exactly one other person. Its leading diagonal counts redundant links: a message that comes back to the sender.
  • T = C + C² counts all one- and two-step links. A 0 off the diagonal of T means that person can’t be reached in two steps or fewer.
  • Everything is calculated live in the browser. Names are made up.

Teaching connections

  • Unit 1Using a matrix to store and display information; order of a square matrix; reading element cij.
  • Unit 1Matrix multiplication and powers: C² as “row of C times column of C”.
  • Unit 1Simple communication matrices and their application — one-step, two-step, redundant and total links.
  • Unit 4Leads into communication and dominance matrices (ranking players in a round-robin tournament uses the same D + D² idea).
